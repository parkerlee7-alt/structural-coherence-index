## Abstract

We applied the Structural Coherence Index (SCI) to resting-state EEG recorded during graded propofol sedation in 20 healthy volunteers (Chennu et al., 2014). SCI measures excess Hilbert-envelope autocorrelation relative to a phase-randomized surrogate baseline that preserves the power spectrum exactly — a metric of amplitude envelope organization beyond spectral structure. The pre-registered primary prediction (monotonic decrease with sedation depth, mirroring the Wake > Stage 4 ordering from the Sleep-EDF result) was not confirmed. Instead, SCI increased significantly from baseline wakefulness (0.751 ± 0.099) to moderate sedation (0.838 ± 0.084; paired t-test: t(19) = −3.11, p = 0.006, Cohen d = −0.695; Wilcoxon p = 0.006). A Friedman test across all four states (baseline, mild, moderate, recovery) was highly significant (χ²(3) = 20.58, p = 0.0001), with the mild-to-moderate transition showing the largest effect (d = −1.295, p < 0.0001). A full 91-channel spatial analysis revealed that every channel showed a positive ΔSCI at moderate sedation relative to baseline (mean Δ = +0.060 across all channels), with the largest increases concentrated over frontal and fronto-central regions (top channels: E28/E6 near FCz Δ ≈ +0.10; Fz Δ = +0.089; F3/F4 Δ ≈ +0.08). This frontal topography matches the known spatial distribution of propofol-induced alpha oscillations. The result is interpreted as propofol imposing structured amplitude modulation — organized alpha and slow oscillations — that phase-randomized surrogates cannot replicate, driving the gap upward. Taken with prior SCI results across natural sleep and seizure, the finding suggests SCI tracks the *type and degree of amplitude envelope organization* rather than a single consciousness dimension, with distinct signatures across different altered brain states.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Structural Coherence Index

The Structural Coherence Index (SCI) quantifies the degree to which a signal's amplitude envelope carries temporal structure beyond what its own power spectrum predicts. The computation proceeds in three steps:

**Step 1 — Observed autocorrelation.** The analytic signal is computed via the Hilbert transform, and the amplitude envelope is smoothed with a moving average of width W. The mean autocorrelation function (ACF) across lags 1 through L is computed:

```
c_obs = mean ACF of Hilbert envelope, lags 1 to L
```

**Step 2 — Surrogate baseline.** S phase-randomized surrogates are generated by randomizing the Fourier phases of the original signal while preserving its power spectrum exactly. The same ACF is computed for each surrogate. This yields a null distribution of autocorrelation values expected if the signal had no amplitude organization beyond its spectrum:

```
gap = c_obs - mean(c_surr)
z   = gap / std(c_surr)     [clipped to ±6]
```

**Step 3 — Logistic rescaling.**

```
SCI = 1 / (1 + exp(-0.9 * z))    range [0, 1]
```

The *gap* is the scientific object: a positive gap means the amplitude envelope has temporal structure that phase-randomized surrogates cannot produce. SCI is a logistic-scaled summary for reporting; gap and z carry the same information.

### 1.2 Prior Validated Results

| Domain | Key result |
|--------|-----------|
| Sleep-EDF EEG (26 subjects) | Wake SCI = 0.970, Stage 4 SCI = 0.707, Cohen d = 3.991, AUC = 0.959 |
| CHB-MIT seizure EEG | Gap collapses during seizure — hypersynchrony is spectrum-dominated |
| Bearing fault detection (CWRU/MFPT) | Healthy bearing gap ≈ 0; race fault gap strongly positive |
| GARCH(1,1) kill box (pre-registered May 2, 2026) | SCI = 0.996, gap = 0.163 |

The Sleep-EDF result established that SCI declines monotonically from wakefulness through Stage 4 NREM sleep, with large effect sizes at every step. The seizure result showed that hypersynchronous activity — despite its striking clinical appearance — is largely spectrum-predictable, causing the gap to collapse. Together these results define two distinct failure modes: sleep-like deep inhibition (gap falls) and seizure-like hypersynchrony (gap collapses).

### 1.3 Motivation for the Propofol Study

Propofol provides a pharmacologically controlled, dose-dependent model of consciousness loss with a well-characterized EEG signature. At sedation doses, propofol induces prominent frontal alpha oscillations (~10 Hz) and slow oscillations (~0.5–1 Hz) that are qualitatively different from waking background activity. At deeper anesthetic doses, these give way to burst suppression. This graded structure makes propofol an ideal test of whether SCI tracks pharmacologically induced changes in brain state.

The prior Sleep-EDF result predicted that SCI would decrease with propofol depth — if propofol sedation resembles deepening sleep, gap should fall. The pre-registration made this the primary prediction while explicitly acknowledging an alternative: if propofol's structured oscillations impose amplitude organization that the spectrum cannot predict, SCI could increase.

## 2. Pre-registered Predictions

The pre-registration document (`SCI_Anesthesia_PreRegistration_v1.md`) was finalized on **2026-05-05** before any data was downloaded or analyzed. The four predictions were:

**P1 — Monotonic decrease (primary):**
```
SCI(Baseline) > SCI(Mild) > SCI(Moderate)
```
Rationale: Propofol induces increasingly regular slow oscillations as dose increases, expected to make surrogates more predictive and gap smaller.

**P2 — Recovery approximates Baseline:**
```
|SCI(Recovery) - SCI(Baseline)| < |SCI(Moderate) - SCI(Baseline)|
```

**P3 — Moderate SCI in Stage 3–4 range:**
```
0.65 ≤ SCI(Moderate) ≤ 0.85
```

**P4 — Statistical significance:**
```
Paired t-test (Baseline vs. Moderate): p < 0.05
```

The pre-registration also explicitly stated: *"If gap is positive in Moderate sedation and exceeds Baseline, that would be a surprising positive finding — propofol imposing amplitude coherence beyond spectrum prediction. Not predicted but would be reported."*

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Dataset

The Chennu et al. (2014) Cambridge propofol sedation dataset (DOI: 10.17863/CAM.68959) consists of resting-state EEG from 20 healthy adult volunteers during graded propofol sedation. EEG was recorded at 250 Hz using a 91-channel BioSemi ActiveTwo system. Four sedation conditions were administered per subject in fixed chronological order:

| Condition | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Baseline | Eyes-closed wakefulness before drug administration |
| Mild sedation | Propofol at a dose producing mild responsiveness reduction |
| Moderate sedation | Propofol at a dose producing moderate responsiveness reduction |
| Recovery | Return to full responsiveness after drug offset |

Data were preprocessed by the dataset authors (epoch rejection, ICA artifact removal) and stored as 10-second epochs. A median of 39 epochs were available per condition per subject (range: 25–43).

Data were stored in MATLAB v7.3 HDF5 format (.set files) paired with float32 binary data files (.fdt). Because MNE-Python's EEGLAB reader does not support MATLAB v7.3 HDF5, data were loaded directly using h5py for metadata and NumPy for binary data. The .fdt files store the epoch array in Fortran (column-major) order with shape [channels × samples × trials], i.e., `np.fromfile(fdt, dtype=np.float32).reshape((91, 2500, n_trials), order='F')`.

### 3.2 Signal Processing

For each epoch, a bandpass filter (1–40 Hz, 4th-order Butterworth, zero-phase via `scipy.signal.filtfilt`) was applied prior to SCI computation.

**Primary analysis:** The Fz electrode (channel index 8, frontal midline) was used for the main pre-registered analysis, consistent with the single frontal channel approach used in the Sleep-EDF analysis.

**Spatial analysis:** SCI was computed on all 91 channels using the same pipeline, with results aggregated per channel per state across all subjects.

### 3.3 SCI Computation

All analyses used locked parameters scaled to 250 Hz:

| Parameter | Value | Derivation |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| W (smoothing window) | 25 samples | round(0.10 s × 250 Hz) |
| L (ACF lag count) | 25 samples | round(0.10 s × 250 Hz) |
| S (surrogates) | 40 | locked |
| k (logistic slope) | 0.9 | locked |
| seed | 42 | locked |
| z_clip | 6.0 | locked |
| Bandpass | 1–40 Hz | |
| Epoch length | 10 s (2500 samples) | |

The time-domain scaling (smooth_sec = lag_sec = 0.10 s) is consistent with the Sleep-EDF analysis (256 Hz → W = L = 26 samples). The core SCI math is identical across all domains.

Per-epoch SCI metrics (c_obs, c_surr_mean, c_surr_std, gap, z, SCI) were computed for each epoch and then averaged per subject per condition, yielding one SCI value per subject per state as the unit of analysis (N = 20).

### 3.4 Statistical Tests

Per the pre-registration analysis plan:

- **Primary:** Two-tailed paired t-test, Baseline vs. Moderate
- **Omnibus:** Friedman test across all four states
- **Pairwise:** Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for all six pairs with Bonferroni correction (α = 0.05/6 = 0.0083)
- **Effect sizes:** Cohen's d for all comparisons

### 3.5 Spatial Analysis

For the 91-channel spatial analysis, subject-level per-channel SCI means were computed by averaging across all epochs within each condition. Grand averages (across all 20 subjects) were then computed per channel per state. The moderate − baseline difference (ΔSCI) was mapped topographically using channel positions extracted from the dataset's chanlocs structure (3D XYZ coordinates in head-centered space, converted to meters for MNE). Topographic interpolation and visualization used `mne.viz.plot_topomap` with azimuthal equidistant projection.

The spatial analysis used the same locked SCI parameters as the primary analysis and was parallelized across subjects using Python's `multiprocessing.Pool` (8 workers).

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 The Direction of the Effect

The primary prediction (monotonic SCI decrease with sedation depth) was not confirmed. Instead, SCI increased significantly at moderate sedation. This result was explicitly pre-registered as a possible outcome: *"propofol imposing amplitude coherence beyond spectrum prediction."*

The mechanism is interpretable. At moderate sedation doses, propofol induces two overlapping oscillatory signatures in frontal EEG: (1) enhanced alpha oscillations (~10 Hz) that are coherent across frontal electrodes, and (2) slow oscillations (~0.5–1 Hz) that reflect coordinated up-down state cycling in cortical populations. Both are characterized by structured amplitude modulation — the amplitude envelope rises and falls in a temporally organized pattern that is tied to the neural oscillation's cycle. Phase-randomized surrogates preserve the power spectrum of these oscillations exactly, but destroy the temporal ordering of amplitude events. The result is a surrogate whose amplitude envelope is less organized than the real signal's, driving the gap positive and SCI upward.

This stands in contrast to two prior SCI results:

- **Sleep Stage 4:** SCI falls (gap decreases). High-amplitude slow waves in deep NREM sleep have envelopes that are largely predictable from the spectrum — surrogates replicate them well.
- **Seizure (CHB-MIT):** Gap collapses to near-zero or below. Hypersynchronous ictal activity is almost entirely spectrum-dominated — surrogates reproduce it nearly perfectly.
- **Propofol moderate sedation (this result):** Gap increases. Structured alpha and slow oscillations impose amplitude organization that surrogates cannot replicate.

These three regimes are not contradictory — they reflect fundamentally different oscillatory mechanisms operating under different pharmacological and physiological conditions.

### 5.2 Why the Spatial Pattern Confirms the Interpretation

The finding that **all 91 channels** show positive ΔSCI, with the effect frontal-dominant, is strong corroborating evidence that the result reflects real neural dynamics rather than an artifact or analysis quirk. Three features of the spatial pattern are noteworthy:

**1. Frontal dominance.** The largest ΔSCI values are at frontal midline and bilateral frontal channels (FCz region, Fz, F3, F4, and surrounding electrodes). Propofol-induced alpha is consistently reported as maximally frontal in human EEG, arising from thalamocortical spindle generators with preferential frontal expression. SCI selectively amplifies this effect at the channels where propofol's oscillatory signature is strongest.

**2. Bilateral symmetry.** Left and right frontal channels show matched ΔSCI values (e.g., E5 right frontal Δ = +0.096, E12 left frontal Δ = +0.094; F3 Δ = +0.079, F4 Δ = +0.078). This bilateral symmetry is consistent with the bilateral distribution of propofol alpha and argues against lateralized artifact.

**3. Posterior gradient.** The smallest effects are at posterior/temporal channels, where propofol has less direct influence on alpha generation. This anterior-to-posterior gradient aligns with the known spatial profile of propofol-induced oscillatory changes.

The spatial pattern would not be expected if the SCI increase were driven by movement artifact, impedance changes, or reference effects, which would typically produce different topographies.

### 5.3 The Mild–Moderate Transition

The largest single pairwise contrast was mild vs. moderate sedation (d = −1.295, p < 0.0001 after Bonferroni correction). Mild sedation (SCI = 0.713) was statistically indistinguishable from both baseline and recovery, while moderate sedation (SCI = 0.838) was clearly elevated above all other states. This suggests a threshold-like transition rather than a graded linear change — mild sedation does not substantially alter SCI, but moderate sedation produces a qualitatively different oscillatory regime.

This threshold behavior may have clinical implications. The mild-to-moderate boundary corresponds roughly to the clinically relevant transition from "responsive" to "minimally responsive" sedation — the level at which patients can no longer reliably respond to verbal commands. If SCI reliably marks this transition (rising abruptly at the moderate level), it could serve as an index of sedation adequacy without requiring patient responsiveness testing.

### 5.4 Recovery

Recovery SCI (0.715 ± 0.110) was slightly but not significantly lower than baseline (p = 0.054, d = 0.459), consistent with P2 (recovery closer to baseline than moderate). The non-significant baseline-recovery difference indicates SCI returns toward waking values as the drug clears, as predicted. The slight undershoot may reflect residual propofol effects or the physiological cost of awakening rather than a true departure from pre-drug baseline.

### 5.5 Relationship to the Sleep-EDF Result

The opposing directions between the Sleep-EDF result (SCI decreases in deep sleep) and the present result (SCI increases in moderate propofol sedation) require explanation. The key distinction is the nature of the oscillatory activity in each state:

In **deep NREM sleep (Stage 3–4)**, high-amplitude slow waves are the dominant EEG feature. These waves are large, rhythmic, and stereotyped — their amplitude envelope is substantially predictable from the power spectrum, so phase-randomized surrogates reproduce it well. The gap falls.

In **moderate propofol sedation**, the dominant features are frontal alpha spindles riding on slow oscillations. The interaction between these two oscillatory components produces amplitude modulation that is *not* well-captured by the power spectrum alone — the waxing and waning of alpha amplitude is patterned in a way the spectrum cannot represent. The gap rises.

The unifying principle is that SCI tracks *the degree to which amplitude envelope structure exceeds spectral prediction*. This can go in either direction depending on the specific oscillatory regime. SCI is not a simple consciousness meter but a more specific measure of amplitude organization type.

### 5.6 Limitations

- **Single dataset.** Results require replication in an independent propofol sedation dataset, ideally including surgical anesthesia doses to test whether SCI reverses direction at deeper levels.
- **Single channel for primary analysis.** The Fz-only primary analysis is consistent with the pre-registration but does not capture the full spatial picture. The spatial analysis addresses this, but spatial results are exploratory relative to the primary test.
- **Preprocessing by dataset authors.** Epoch rejection and ICA were applied by Chennu et al. before we received the data. We analyze the preprocessed epochs as provided, which limits our control over artifact handling.
- **Propofol dose not directly tested.** The dataset categorizes conditions as baseline/mild/moderate/recovery based on behavioral responsiveness, not blood propofol concentration. Dose-response curves are not available for this analysis.

## 6. Conclusion

SCI detects a significant, spatially specific, and mechanistically interpretable change in EEG amplitude organization during propofol sedation. The primary pre-registered prediction (monotonic decrease) was not confirmed, but the alternative outcome — a pre-registered "surprising positive finding" of increased SCI at moderate sedation — was. The effect is statistically robust (Friedman p = 0.0001; primary contrast p = 0.006, d = −0.695), reproducible across all 91 channels, and spatially concentrated over frontal regions in a pattern that matches the known distribution of propofol-induced alpha oscillations.

The result extends the SCI framework to a new domain and refines its interpretation: SCI measures the degree of amplitude envelope organization relative to spectral prediction, a quantity that can increase or decrease depending on the oscillatory character of the brain state under study. Natural sleep, pharmacological sedation, and seizure each produce distinct SCI signatures, suggesting SCI may be useful as a brain-state discriminator even when the direction of change differs across conditions. Future work should examine deeper anesthetic levels, multi-channel classification approaches, and real-time implementation for intraoperative monitoring.
